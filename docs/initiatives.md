# Initiatives

An **initiative** is a place to drive one thing to ship: the Jira epic, the Slack threads, the meetings, the decisions and the docs around it. It is delivery-focused, not a metrics dashboard. Beluga's "projects" keep their meaning (a repository); an initiative links to some of them as its repos.

The agent **suggests, the user approves**. It never writes to Jira, Slack or the calendar on its own: what it would do outward goes into `suggestions.json`, and approving one sends it into the initiative's chat, where the outward tool asks as any tool does.

## 1. The memory repo

One git repository, one folder per initiative. Its path is kept in `~/.beluga/initiatives.json` (`{ "root": "~/…" }`), `~/Documents/GitHub/initiatives` by default. Beluga runs `git init` on first use (with a `README.md` and a `.gitignore` that leaves out `.beluga/`). Without a remote it only commits. When the branch tracks one (`git push -u`), every commit is pushed right after; a push that fails (offline, no access) leaves the commit local and goes out with the next one. A failing push shows as a warning on the Initiatives page (`backup`: when it first failed and git's last error) until a push goes through. When the daemon starts, it pushes commits still waiting.

```
initiatives/
├── README.md
├── people/               the people directory, shared by every initiative (§10)
│   └── <person>.json     name, cares, slack, jira, notes
└── <slug>/
    ├── initiative.json   name, goal, status, epics, slack, people ({ person, role }), meetings, docs, repos, audiences
    ├── brief.md          where it stands now: every chat starts from it
    ├── decisions.md      the agent's notes: "## Open" (waiting on someone else) and "## Decided", one dated bullet each
    ├── timeline.md       what happened, newest first, one bullet each ("- 2026-11-25 [check-in]: …")
    ├── people.md         who is involved and what they own
    ├── status.json       epic progress from the last check-in
    ├── tickets.json      every ticket under the epics and their sub-tasks, with their pull requests
    ├── tasks.json        your Google Tasks that belong to it, scheduled or not
    ├── suggestions.json  what the agent proposes: open, approved or dismissed
    ├── watches.json      follow-ups the agent keeps looking for between check-ins
    ├── artifacts.json    what worktrees sent it (report_to_initiative), newest last
    ├── risks.json        what could sink it: the agent keeps it, you edit it on the page
    ├── artifacts/        their files, `<id>-<name>`
    ├── updates.json      stakeholder updates sent (and prep sheets kept), with the tickets as they stood (§11)
    ├── updates/          each one's text, `<day>-<audience>-<draft>.md`
    └── sources/          snapshots: jira.md, slack/, meetings/, docs/, prs.md
```

- **The folder is the initiative.** `initiative.json` is the source of truth; the daemon reads it with zod (`InitiativeConfigSchema`, `model.ts`). A file that doesn't parse shows as the initiative's trouble on its card and page, with the folder's name as its name; nothing crashes, and editing it waits until it's fixed.
- **Slug.** The folder's name, from the name (`Checkout v2` → `checkout-v2`, `-2` when taken): lowercase letters, digits and hyphens, 40 at most.
- **What it follows.** `slack` is a list of `{ ref, label? }`: a `#channel`, or the link of a thread or a DM (500 characters at most). `meetings` is a list of `{ match, label? }`: words a meeting's title has, or one event's link or id. A file from before BLG-114 with `slackChannels` (strings) and `calendarMatch` (one string) still reads: they become `slack` and `meetings` entries on read, and the next save writes the new shape. The goal is Markdown, 12000 characters at most.
- **status.json**: `{ at, total, done, inProgress, blocked, stale, epics: [{ key, title, total, done, inProgress, blocked, stale }] }`. "Last check-in" is its `at`.
- **tickets.json**: `{ at, epics: [{ key, url?, title? }], tickets: [{ key, url?, summary, status, statusCategory (todo | inProgress | done), assignee?, updated?, epic?, parent?, prs: [{ url, number?, state (open | draft | merged | closed), repo?, title? }] }] }` (`TicketsSchema`). The agent writes it, so the read forgives how a state is spelled (Jira's `new` and `indeterminate`, `MERGED`, a `null` for nothing); a file that still doesn't parse is the initiative's trouble, and its table stays empty. `epics` gives each epic's Jira link and title; without one, the page builds the link from a ticket's (`epicUrlOf`: its `/browse/KEY` with the epic's key), and an epic with neither stays plain text. A sub-task carries its parent ticket's key in `parent`: the table shows it indented under that ticket, which shows `done/total sub-tasks`, and the line above counts sub-tasks apart from tickets. A sub-task whose parent wasn't read shows as a ticket of its own. `links` (optional) holds a ticket's Jira "Blocks" links, `[{ type (blocks | blockedBy), key, summary?, status?, statusCategory, url? }]`, including links to tickets outside the epics; `is blocked by` reads as `blockedBy`, and any other link type is dropped on read.
- **risks.json**: `[{ id, title, why?, severity (high | medium | low), owner?, mitigation?, state (open | watching | closed), sources: [{ label, url? }], blocker?, createdAt?, updatedAt?, closedAt?, owned? }]` (`RisksSchema`, `risks.ts`). A missing file reads as no risks. Severity and state are read forgivingly (`High` is high, anything unknown is medium and open). `owned` is the owner's edit from the page (see §12, Risks and blockers).
- **tasks.json**: `{ at, tasks: [{ id, title, notes?, list?, due?, scheduled?, done, pending? (complete | reopen), url? }] }` (`TasksSchema`): the Google Tasks the agent keeps for this initiative, read through the calendar MCP. `scheduled` is the start of its first calendar placement; `pending` is a tick from the page on its way to Google.
- **outbox.json**: the messages drafted for you to send (see §5, The outbox).
- **decisions.md**: the agent's file; the daemon only reads it (`decisionsOf`, `src/core/initiatives/decisions.ts`), as it reads `timeline.md`. "## Open" holds the questions that wait on someone else, "- 2026-11-25: the question, who it waits on and since when"; "## Decided" the settled ones, "- 2026-11-25: what was decided. Why: the reason", ending in "(suggestion N)" when it records a suggestion (`DECISIONS_SHAPE`, `prompt.ts`). The read forgives older lines: a day in brackets at the end ("(raised 2026-11-25)"), a bold day, a line wrapped onto an indented one, and plain lines grouping the bullets under them ("Waiting on stakeholders:"). A file with neither heading gives no `decisions` and the page only lists it among the memory files, as before. Existing files aren't rewritten: the next check-in writes the new shape as it touches them.
- **watches.json**: the follow-ups it waits for (see §5, Follow-ups).
- **suggestions.json**: `[{ id, text, why?, kind (jira | slack | calendar | task | doc | decision | other), options?, answer?, state (open | approved | dismissed; an answered decision is approved, and an agent's `decided` reads as approved), createdAt?, resolvedAt? }]`. The page lists the open ones.
- **artifacts.json**: `[{ id, title, why?, file? (in `artifacts/`), path? (kept off the repo: where it is on this computer), name, bytes, ticket?, worktree?, project?, createdAt }]` (`ArtifactsSchema`). Beluga writes it, never the agent; see §8 (Artifacts).
- **Commits.** After an initiative is made or changed, after a suggestion is resolved, and after each turn in one of its chats (its done, error or denied event), the daemon commits that folder alone if anything in it changed: `<slug>: created`, `<slug>: updated`, `<slug>: suggestion 3 approved`, `<slug>: check-in 2026-11-24`, `<slug>: chat 2026-11-24`. Commits queue per repo, never sign (`commit.gpgsign=false`), and fall back to "Beluga <beluga@localhost>" when git has no identity.
- **Not in the repo.** Chat histories and agent sessions stay in `~/.beluga/initiatives/<slug>/`: `chats.json`, `chats/<id>/history.json` and `state.json`, and `local.json` (the ids of its check-in and watch sentinels, which are this computer's). Search reads the histories (§2).

## 2. Chats

Each initiative has several chats, like the Sentinel chat (docs/sentinel.md §12): `main` first, and `checkin`, the one its check-in wakes, and `watch` (Follow-ups) once its watch sentinel first wakes it. The channel is `initiative:<slug>:<chatId>` (`initiativeChatChannel`, `model.ts`); the protocol's `Channel`, chat refs (`shared/chat-ref.ts`) and owner-only posting (`core/workspace-sharing.ts`) know it, so only you write there and a sentinel's chat step can name it (`<you>/initiative:<slug>:checkin`).

- **Where it runs.** The initiative's folder is the agent's working folder, with `autoEdit` (files it writes there need no approval), and its own resumable session per chat (`InitiativeChats.place`, `chats.ts`).
- **What it is told.** `initiativeBrief` (`prompt.ts`, the run instructions' `initiative` piece): what an initiative is, its epics, every Slack conversation and meeting it follows with how to read each (a channel since the last check-in, a thread by its link with the Slack MCP's `slack_read_thread`, a meeting by title words or by its event), every doc source with how to read it and where its snapshot goes, its goal and `brief.md` inline (each in tags it can't break out of), the other memory files by name, the linked repos, and the rules: keep the memory current, record decisions, suggest instead of acting outward.
- **Board tools.** Your own runs get the task board as anywhere (`BoardWork.forRun`): `task_create` with `project` set to a linked repo's name, then `task_start` when you want it started. The chats manage; they don't code.
- **New, open, delete.** `initiatives.chatNew`, `chatOpen`, `chatDelete` (its run stops first; its messages, folder and agent transcript go). The Check-in chat can't be deleted.
- **Search.** The Search page and your agents' `search_chats` read every initiative chat (`Workbench.searchSources`): the ones in the log, and from `history.json` those that aren't (`Initiatives.savedChats`). A hit's place is "<initiative> › <chat title>"; opening it brings the chat's history back into the log if it has none there and opens the initiative's page on that chat. A chat whose initiative is gone is left out.
- **Honcho.** What you type in an initiative chat, and your agent's answers there, go to Honcho like a worktree's (docs/honcho.md): one session per chat, kind `initiative`, titled with the initiative's name. What the page writes into the chat for you (a check-in, a refresh, "Do suggestion N", "Send draft N", a task tick) is marked `written: 'initiative'` and stays here, as a sentinel's prompt does. Sentinel chats stay out of search's places and Honcho for now.

## 3. The check-in

A check-in is a sentinel, not new machinery (docs/sentinel.md §13). Making an initiative makes one: `<name> check-in`, a `schedule` trigger on weekdays at 08:00 until you set another time (catch-up when missed), access Look, and one `chat` step that sends `CHECK_IN_PROMPT` into the initiative's Check-in chat. Like every new sentinel it starts paused; the page's switch turns it on (`initiatives.setCheckIn`, which makes it again if it was deleted). The row under the switch sets its time and weekdays (`initiatives.setCheckInTime`, which rewrites the sentinel's trigger, so the sentinel stays the only place the schedule lives; editing it on the Sentinel page shows here too). **Check In Now** runs it once (`initiatives.checkInNow`; without Sentinel, it sends the prompt straight into the Check-in chat).

The prompt has the agent read the epics' tickets (Jira MCP), every Slack conversation it follows, the calendar a week each way for every meeting it follows (prep notes for one that is coming, what was said in one that happened, below), every doc source, the linked repos' pull requests (gh) and your Google Tasks, then update `tickets.json`, `tasks.json`, `sources/`, `status.json`, `brief.md`, `timeline.md`, `decisions.md` and `suggestions.json`, and answer with one line on how it stands. The turn ending commits the folder. The Check-in chat's instructions carry the current prompt too, so a check-in sentinel made by an older version still does the whole of it.

**The tickets.** `TICKETS_PROMPT` (`prompt.ts`) is the ticket part alone: every issue under each epic (`jira_search` with `parent = KEY`) and then their sub-tasks (`parent in (…)`), each one's pull requests from Jira's development info and from `gh pr list --search KEY` in the linked repos, written to `tickets.json`, with `status.json`'s counts set from the same tickets. The daemon never calls Jira: it sends that prompt into the Check-in chat when an initiative is made with epics, when its epics change, and on the page's Refresh (`initiatives.refreshTickets`).

**The tasks.** `TASKS_PROMPT` is the task part alone: `list_tasks` (open, and done in the last two weeks) through the calendar MCP, keeping the tasks whose title or notes name the initiative, its goal, an epic or a ticket, and every task the agent made for it, written whole to `tasks.json`. The page's Tasks › Refresh sends it (`initiatives.refreshTasks`). Ticking a task (`initiatives.setTaskDone`) marks it `pending` in `tasks.json` at once, commits, and sends "Complete task ID: …" (or "Reopen task ID: …") into the initiative's open chat; the brief tells the agent to read it with `get_task` for its version, call `complete_task` or `reopen_task`, then set `done` and drop `pending`. Asking the chat to tick one off does the same. The daemon never calls calendar-api itself.

**Doc sources.** `docs` in `initiative.json` is a list of `{ ref, label?, kind }`; `kind` (`confluence`, `gdoc`, `gsheet`, `gslides` or `link`) is worked out from the link (`docKindOf`, `model.ts`) when it isn't given, and an old file's plain string links become entries on read. The brief names each one with how to read it (`docLine`, `prompt.ts`): a Confluence page with the Confluence MCP's `confluence_get_page` by its page id (mcp-atlassian with `CONFLUENCE_URL`, `CONFLUENCE_USERNAME` and `CONFLUENCE_API_TOKEN` set), a Google Doc, Sheet or Slides with the calendar MCP's `drive_read_file` by its file id (calendar-api on the NAS holds both the work and the private Google account; claude.ai's Drive connector holds only one, so it's only the fallback), any other link with whatever tool can open it. Each has a snapshot at `sources/docs/<kind>-<page or file id>.md`; the check-in compares what it reads with it, writes the new one and notes in `timeline.md` what changed.

**Meetings.** `MEETINGS_HOW` (`meetings.ts`, a part of `CHECK_IN_PROMPT`) reads each followed meeting held since the last check-in (`status.json`'s `at`, or a week back), never the rest of the calendar. It looks for notes in this order: the event's attachments and description (the calendar MCP's `get_event`), Drive (`drive_search` with the title and date for the "Notes by Gemini" doc and the Meet transcript, then `drive_read_file`), then Beluga call notes (`call_notes_search`, `call_notes_read`; your own runs have them, initiative chats included). A doc too big for one tool result is saved by Claude Code under `~/.claude/projects/…/tool-results/`; the agent Reads it with `offset` and `limit` until the end. That Read needs no new rule: an initiative chat is your own run at full reach, so a file outside the shared folders is allowed (`tests/initiatives-meetings.test.ts` pins it). It writes `sources/meetings/<date>-<title>.md` (`meetingNotesFile`): who was there, a summary, decisions, action items, asks and the links the notes came from. Transcripts may hold things said in confidence, so only that summary is kept, never the raw transcript. Then:
- a decision made in the meeting is a new suggestion that is already decided: kind `decision`, `answer` what was decided, `state` `approved`, `resolvedAt` the meeting, `outcome` "Decided in <title> on <date>", the meeting's notes in `sources`. It was settled in the room, not the user's call, so it shows in the decision log as Decided (`logStateOf`) rather than as an open question; it is the one place the agent writes a state itself. An open decision the meeting settled keeps its state; its `why` says how the meeting settled it.
- an action item for you is a `task` suggestion ("Create a task: …"), never a task made on its own;
- what someone else agreed to do is waiting on them: a dated bullet under `## Open` in `decisions.md` ending in `(waiting on: <name>)` ("- 2026-11-26: the final tax table, by Friday (Go/no-go) (waiting on: Natalia)"), so the page lists it among the questions waiting on others and People counts it as waiting on them (§10), and in `people.md` and as a timeline line.
- a meeting with no notes anywhere gets `<day> [meeting]: <title> held, no notes found (looked in the event, Drive and call notes)` (`noNotesLine`) and the check-in carries on.

An initiative that follows no meetings gets one `calendar` suggestion listing recurring meetings whose title names it, an epic or its people; the brief says so (`meetingsHint`), and for one that follows meetings it names where their notes are. The demo's Checkout v2 has a meeting-made decision, a task from the design review, a Jonas "waiting on" and a no-notes timeline line.

## 4. Refining the goal

`initiatives.refineGoal({ name, goal, epics, docs? })` → `{ goal }` is a side call (`agent/goal-refiner.ts`, like naming a worktree): one run with no session and no files, which returns Markdown (what shipped looks like, then Background, Scope, Acceptance and Open questions, each only when there is something true to say) and writes nowhere. On Claude it mounts only your own Jira server (a server of yours named like `jira` or `atlassian`, from Claude Code's files in your home folder) and allows only its Jira and Confluence reads; with a Google file among the docs it also mounts your `calendar` server and allows only its `drive_read_file`, `drive_get_file` and `drive_search` (`sourceReadVerdict`). With any doc attached it also gets Read, allowed only for a file Claude Code saved under its `projects/…/tool-results/` because a source was too long to answer inline (a Doc with images can be, as the gateway keeps them). Every other tool, and every write, is refused. Another runtime drafts from what was typed. The sheet and the page show the draft with Use This and Keep Mine; only Use This on the page saves, through `initiatives.update`.

## 5. Approving a suggestion

`initiatives.resolveSuggestion(slug, id, 'approve' | 'dismiss')` marks it in `suggestions.json` (with `resolvedAt`) and commits. Approving sends "Do suggestion N: <text>" into the initiative's open chat (its `main` when the Check-in chat is open). The run then goes through the normal approval path for any outward tool (a Jira comment, a Slack post), so nothing new is trusted.

A suggestion that is the user's call is a decision: `"kind": "decision"`, with `"options"` (2 to 4 short answers) the page shows as buttons, plus Other… for an answer of their own. `resolveSuggestion(slug, id, 'approve', answer)` keeps the `answer` on it and sends "Decided suggestion N: <text>\nAnswer: <answer>"; the chat records it in `decisions.md` under "## Decided" and does what follows from it. `decisions.md` is the agent's own notes: the prompt has every open question only the user can settle also be a decision suggestion (one per question), and a question that only waits on someone else stay in `decisions.md`, named in a decision's `why` when it bears on it; the page lists those (below). A suggestion of another kind that has `options` is a decision too.

**Clarify in chat.** `initiatives.clarifySuggestion(slug, id)` → `{ channel }` keeps an open suggestion open, sets its `clarifiedAt` and sends "Clarify suggestion N: <text>\nWhy: <why>" into the same chat Approve would use; the page switches to that chat. The brief tells the agent to explain it with the tickets, messages and people behind it, answer follow-ups and not act on it; when the talk changes it, the agent rewrites its `text` or `why`. The chat is never inline on the page: the chat window is where the talk happens.

**The decision log.** The page's Suggestions section (`SuggestionLog.tsx`) is full-width rows: *Needs you* (the open ones, "In chat" on those being clarified), *Waiting on others* (`decisions.md`'s "## Open" bullets with their day and the plain lines that group them, its words linked as the timeline's are; `DecisionNotes.tsx`; not there when the file has another shape) and the *Decision log* (`decisionLog`, `decisions.ts`: every resolved one, with Approved · Decided · Dismissed and how many proofs, and "## Decided"'s notes as Note · Decided rows with their day and "Why:", the latest first). A note that records a suggestion the log already shows is left out: the one it names with "(suggestion N)", or, for an older line without it, the decision resolved within a day of it that shares the most words with it, two at least, leaving out words more than two notes use (the initiative's own vocabulary); each decision pairs with one note at most (`recordedNotes`). Both lists use the same rows: the kind, badges and date on top, the suggestion below in up to two lines, so it stays readable when the panel makes the page narrow. A click on a row opens it as a record in the right panel, in a tab beside Chat named after its kind and number (Decision #2, × closes it; `InitiativePanel.tsx`), and Clarify in chat goes back to the Chat tab: Context (`why`), Sources (`sourcesOf`: the `sources` the agent named on it, `[{ label, url? }]`, then every ticket key its words mention from a project the initiative knows, `RUN-204/205` read as two, linked to Jira like the epics, then the links in it, and the initiative's docs and Slack channels it names by label), then for an open one its actions (a decision's answers, Dismiss, Clarify in chat, Approve), and for a resolved one Decision (`answer`, or how it was resolved, with the date), Outcome and Proof, with Copy as Markdown (`recordMarkdown`, `src/core/initiatives/log.ts`). After doing an approved or decided suggestion the agent writes its `outcome` (one line) and `proof` (`[{ label, url? }]`: the Jira comment, the Slack permalink, the pull request) on it in `suggestions.json`; every outbox draft from it the user sent is proof too (`proofOf`). Only http(s) links open.

### The outbox

A message (a Slack post or thread reply, a Jira comment) never leaves an initiative's chat on the agent's word. The agent drafts it in `outbox.json`: `[{ id, to, text, why?, suggestion?, state (draft | approved | sent | discarded), sentText?, createdAt?, resolvedAt? }]`, where `to` is `{ kind: 'slack', channel, thread?, label? }`, `{ kind: 'jira', issue, label? }`, `{ kind: 'confluence', page, section, label? }` (one section of a page, by its id and heading) or `{ kind: 'prep', meeting, label? }` (a prep sheet, never sent), and `audience` marks a stakeholder update (§11) (`OutboxDraftSchema`). Slack messages and Jira comments take 5000 characters, a Confluence section or a prep sheet 20000. The chat shows the open drafts above its message box, one card at a time (`OutboxCards`): where it goes, why, the text in an editable field, and Send · Discard · Later.

Send (`initiatives.sendDraft(slug, id, text)`) marks it `approved` (keeping the edit as `sentText`, the agent's `text` as it was), keeps that one call in the daemon's memory (never in a file the agent writes), and asks the chat to make it: "Send draft N from the outbox now: <tool> with <ids>, and the message exactly as below". The chat's run gets a `vetTool` and a `preApproved` from `chatRun`: any `slack_send_message`, `conversations_add_message` (the slack-user MCP, text in `text`), `slack_schedule_message`, `jira_add_comment` or `confluence_update_page_section` call (page id, the heading exactly and `new_content`, in Markdown) (whatever the MCP server is called) is refused unless it matches a sent draft exactly (tool, channel and thread or issue, and the text, trimmed); a match is let through once, with no second card (the Send was the approval), and the draft becomes `sent` with a line in `timeline.md`. Discard (`initiatives.discardDraft`) marks it `discarded`, also with a line. An approved draft the chat didn't post (the daemon restarted, the chat was busy) shows "Send Again". External agents can't send: initiatives are refused to them.

### Follow-ups

After you send a draft, the agent keeps looking for the answer by itself between check-ins, and tells you briefly when something new came in (`watches.ts`, `watching.ts`, `watch-prompt.ts`, `watch-notices.ts`).

- **watches.json**: `[{ id, what, where, waitingOn?, suggestion?, draft?, since, every?, lastLook?, until?, nudgedAt?, state (watching | answered | stopped | expired), news: [{ at, from?, text, read }] }]` (`WatchesSchema`). `where` is a Slack conversation or thread (`{ kind: 'slack', channel, thread?, label? }`, the outbox's shape), a Jira ticket (`{ kind: 'jira', issue, label? }`), a doc (`{ kind: 'doc', ref, label? }`) or a pull request (`{ kind: 'pr', url, label? }`). `waitingOn` is a plain name. A folder without the file has none.
- **Made three ways.** Sending a draft (`markSent`) adds a watch on its target with the draft and its suggestion. The brief tells the agent to add one when an approved suggestion waits on someone, or when you ask it in chat. The page's Waiting for › Watch for an Answer… adds one (`initiatives.addWatch(slug, { what, where, waitingOn?, suggestion?, every? })`; the field takes a Jira key, a Slack link, a pull request or any link, `watchPlaceOf`).
- **Cadence.** Without `every` (hours) a watch backs off: hourly the first day, every four hours until the third, daily after (`watchEvery`), from its last look. Looks happen on weekdays from 08:00 to 18:00 local time (`workingTime`): a look due at night or on a weekend waits for the next morning.
- **The watch sentinel.** One per initiative, beside the check-in: `<name> follow-ups`, access Look, notify off, one `chat` step into the initiative's Follow-ups chat (`watch`, made the first time it is woken). Its trigger is `once`: the daemon reads `watches.json` and arms it for the earliest next look (`nextWakeAt`), with a prompt (`watchPrompt`) naming only the watches due then and only their places; with nothing to watch it pauses. It re-arms after a watch is added, stopped or read, after every turn in one of the initiative's chats (the agent may have changed the file), when the status changes (an archived one watches nothing) and when the daemon starts. So no agent turn is spent while nothing is due, and the sentinel still keeps the clock, catches up after sleep and shows its runs. A sentinel's chat step always spends a turn and Sentinel has no gate, which is why the daemon re-arms a `once` trigger instead of a fixed schedule. The daemon never calls Slack or Jira.
- **A look.** When a turn in the Follow-ups chat ends, the daemon sets `lastLook` on the watches it asked about (the due ones, and Look Now's); the agent never writes it. Nothing new: the agent changes no file and answers "Nothing"; no notification (that chat's runs are quiet in `finishedRuns`), no timeline line. Something new: it adds `news` (what came in, from whom, what it changes), a `[follow-up]` line in `timeline.md`, updates `decisions.md` and the suggestion it belongs to, proposes the next step as a suggestion, drafts a reply in the outbox, and sets `answered` when it is. A watch that waited seven days with nothing back gets a nudge drafted into the outbox, once (`nudgedAt`); past `until` (two weeks after `since` by default) the daemon sets it `expired` with a timeline line.
- **Telling you.** New unread news raises a notice (`followUpNews` in `detectNotices`, kind `mentions`, once per item by its key) on the Follow-ups chat's channel; opening it opens the initiative on that chat (`noticeTarget` knows initiative channels).
- **The page.** "New since you asked" (unread news, Mark Read · Mark All Read, `initiatives.readNews(slug, id?)`) and "Waiting for" (each watching one: what, who, where, last look, next look, Look Now (`initiatives.lookNow`: the Follow-ups chat looks at it at once), Stop (`initiatives.stopWatch`)), in `WatchesSection.tsx` with `WatchSheet.tsx`.
- **The check-in** reads `watches.json` too, so news a follow-up already brought is not reported again.

## 6. The API and the state

`src/ipc/initiatives-api.ts` (zod only) and `initiatives-handlers.ts`: `initiatives.create(draft)` → `{ slug }`, `update(slug, patch)`, `archive(slug)` (its check-in pauses), `setRepo(slug, repo, linked)`, `setCheckIn(slug, on)`, `checkInNow(slug)`, `refreshTickets(slug)`, `refineGoal(input)`, `chatNew(slug)`, `chatOpen(slug, id)`, `chatDelete(slug, id)`, `resolveSuggestion(slug, id, decision, answer?)`, `clarifySuggestion(slug, id)`, `sendDraft(slug, id, text)`, `discardDraft(slug, id)`, `addWatch(slug, draft)`, `stopWatch(slug, id)`, `lookNow(slug, id)`, `readNews(slug, id?)`, `readFile(slug, path)` (a memory file, never outside its folder), and for people (§10) `savePerson(draft)` → `{ slug }`, `removePerson(person)`, `setPerson(slug, person, role | null)` and `proposePeople(slug)` → `{ channel }`, and for stakeholder updates (§11) `saveAudience(slug, draft)` → `{ id }`, `removeAudience(slug, id)`, `draftUpdate(slug, id)` → `{ channel }` and `keepPrep(slug, draftId, text)`, and for risks (§12) `saveRisk(slug, draft)`. All are `mac` for the phone (`src/companion/coverage.ts`), `drive` for your other computers, and refused to external agents. Messages go through `session.submit` with the chat's channel.

The state's `initiatives` slice (`InitiativesState`: `enabled`, `root`, `items`, and `backup` while pushes fail) and `people`, the directory) changes through `initiatives_init`, `initiative_put`, `initiative_remove`, `initiatives_backup`, `initiatives_people` and `initiatives_off`. Each item is an `InitiativeView`: the config's fields (`slack`, `meetings` among them), `progress`, `tickets`, `tasks`, `suggestions`, `outbox`, `watches`, `timeline` (`timeline.md`'s bullets parsed as `{ day, kind, text }`, up to 500), `decisions` (`decisions.md` as `{ open, decided }` of `{ day, text, why?, suggestion?, group? }`, up to 200 each; absent when it has neither heading), `waitingQuestions` (§10), `files`, `checkIn` (its sentinel's id), `chats` and `trouble`. The views are `initiatives` (the list), `initiative_page` (`slug`, and `chat`: its channel is the page's conversation) and `initiative_people` (People across initiatives, §10).

## 8. Artifacts

Worktrees send the initiative what their work made: a report, test or validation output, a screenshot as proof, a generated spec.

- **The tool.** Your own runs in a worktree, on every runtime, get `mcp__beluga_initiatives__report_to_initiative` (`file`, `title`, `why?`, `initiative?`; `src/agent/initiative-tools.ts`) when there are initiatives that aren't archived. `src/core/initiatives/report.ts` links the worktree by its ticket (`ws.ticket`, then a key in its branch, then in its name, `ticketKeyIn`): the initiative one of whose epics, tickets or sub-tasks it is (`initiativeOfTicket`, active ones first). Linked, its brief says so and asks for what's worth keeping, never every scratch file; not linked, it lists the initiatives and the agent names one when you ask. A friend's run, a guest's and an external agent's never get it.
- **Kept.** `Initiatives.reportArtifact` copies the file (a path in the worktree, or a full path) into `artifacts/<id>-<name>`, adds it to `artifacts.json` with its ticket, worktree and project, puts "artifact #N “title” from RUN-52" on top of `timeline.md` and commits `<slug>: artifact N`. A file over 50 MB (`ARTIFACT_KEEP_MAX`: GitHub warns there and refuses 100 MB) stays where it is: the entry keeps its `path` and the agent is told to leave it. A secret (`isSecretPath`), a folder or a missing file is refused.
- **The check-in** reads the ones sent since the last one, notes in the timeline what each shows, and raises a suggestion, the artifact among its sources, when one bears on a decision or shows a problem.
- **The page.** The right panel's Artifacts tab (`ArtifactList.tsx`, with its count): the newest first, each with its number, title, why, ticket, worktree, size and when, and "Not in the repo" for one kept off it. A row opens in a sheet (`ArtifactSheet.tsx`, `initiatives.readArtifact`): text drawn (Markdown as Markdown), a picture shown (up to 15 MB), any other file by its path on the daemon's computer, with Copy Path.

## 10. People

Stakeholders are kept once for every initiative, so the same Ops lead never exists three times. They are people outside Beluga (not friends), and the tool stays single-player.

- **The directory.** `people/<slug>.json` at the repo's root (`directory.ts`): `{ name, cares, slack, jira, notes }` (`DirectoryPersonSchema`, `people.ts`), with a `README.md`. The slug comes from the name (`Ömer Kaya` → `omer-kaya`, `-2` when taken). `people` is never an initiative's folder: `listSlugs` skips it and a new initiative named People gets `people-2`. Changes commit as `people: <slug> saved`, `people: <slug> removed`, `people: from <initiative>`.
- **On an initiative.** `initiative.json`'s `people` is `[{ person, role? }]`: a directory slug and their role there. An old `{ name, role }` still reads, as the slug of its name (`PersonSchema`, `model.ts`); the page shows it as Not in the directory until it is edited, which makes its entry under that slug.
- **What is open with someone** is derived, never typed: an open suggestion's `waitingOn` (a slug, a name or a handle: it waits on them, they owe you) and `askedBy` (they wait on you), and an open question in `decisions.md`'s "## Open" that ends with `(waiting on: <name>)` (`waitingQuestionsOf`, on the view as `waitingQuestions`). **Last contact** is the latest `said`, `message` or `meeting` line in `timeline.md` that names them (their full name, a first name of three letters or more, or a handle).
- **The agent.** The brief lists the initiative's people with their roles and the rest of the directory (`peopleBrief`, `people-prompt.ts`), and tells it to set `waitingOn` and `askedBy`, mark open questions, and never write `people/` or `initiative.json`'s people itself: a change is a suggestion with `people`. The check-in prompt (`PEOPLE_CHECK_IN`) says the same.
- **The one-time pass.** `PEOPLE_PASS` asks the Check-in chat to read `people.md` and write one suggestion with `people: [{ name, role, cares, slack, jira, notes, person? }]` (`kind` "other"; an agent's "people" reads as other). The page's People › From people.md sends it (`initiatives.proposePeople`) while the initiative has nobody and no people suggestion yet, and a check-in does it once then too. Nothing is written until you approve it: approving folds each one into the directory (an entry with the same name or `person` keeps what it has and gains what was empty) and onto the initiative with their role, sets its outcome and a timeline line, and sends nothing to the chat. `people.md` stays the agent's free notes.
- **The page.** People (`PeopleSection.tsx`), under Suggestions: one row each with their role, last contact, Slack and Jira, Waiting on them and They wait on you; + picks someone from the directory or New Person…, the pencil edits them and their role (`PersonSheet.tsx`), × takes them off. A suggestion's record shows Waiting on, They wait on you and the people it proposes (`SuggestionPeople.tsx`).
- **People across initiatives** (`PeoplePage.tsx`, the list's People button, or ⋯ › People Across Initiatives): everyone in the directory, those with the most open first, each with their roles per initiative, what they care about, last contact, and everything open with them across the initiatives that aren't archived (`peopleAcross`); New Person, edit, and remove (refused while an initiative names them).

## 11. Stakeholder updates

The agent drafts a status update for each audience of an initiative, on its schedule or when you ask, from what actually changed. You edit and send it, or take it into a meeting. Nothing goes out without you.

- **Audiences.** `initiative.json`'s `audiences` is `[{ id, name, people, tone, cadence, target }]` (`AudienceSchema`, `audience.ts`; none when the file has none, so older folders load as they are): `people` are directory slugs (§10), `tone` is `short` (execs: five lines at most) or `detailed` (the team: What changed, Tickets, Decisions, Open, Next), `cadence` is `{ days, at }` (weekdays, 0 Sunday, and a local time; no days is on request only), and `target` is a Slack channel or thread (`{ kind: 'slack', channel, thread? }`; a pasted message link gives both), a Confluence page's section (`{ kind: 'confluence', page, section }`: the update replaces that heading's body, so the page shows the latest and the folder keeps the history) or a prep sheet for a meeting the initiative follows (`{ kind: 'prep', meeting }`).
- **Due.** An audience is due at the first slot of its cadence after its last update, or, never updated, at its latest slot so far, unless a draft for it is waiting (`isUpdateDue`, `updates.ts`). The daemon works it out; the agent only writes. Every chat's brief lists the audiences with last sent and next due (`audiencesBrief`, `updates-prompt.ts`), and the Check-in chat's marks the due ones DUE with their facts; the check-in prompt ends with drafting those (`UPDATES_CHECK_IN`), so their drafts are on the page after the next check-in.
- **The facts.** Worked out by Beluga for each one (`updateFacts`): the timeline since its last update, the ticket delta against the tickets kept with that update (done, moved, new, reassigned, no longer under the epics: `ticketDelta`), the decisions you made since, and what is open and with whom (open suggestions' `waitingOn` and `askedBy`, and the open questions marked waiting, by the directory's names). Risks and success metrics join when those land.
- **The draft** is an outbox draft with `audience` set and `to` from the target (§5, The outbox), one open per audience. Slack and Confluence drafts go out only through Send, guarded like every draft. A prep sheet is never sent: its card has Copy as Markdown and Done (`initiatives.keepPrep`), and Send refuses it.
- **Kept.** When an update is sent (or a prep sheet kept), Beluga writes its text to `updates/<day>-<audience>-<draft>.md`, adds `{ audience, at, draft, to, file, tickets }` to `updates.json` (the tickets as they stand, the next delta's start), and puts `[update]: sent the Leadership update to #leadership` on top of `timeline.md`. The agent never writes either.
- **The page.** Updates (`UpdatesSection.tsx`), under People: one row per audience with its tone, where it goes and who for, Last sent (Last kept for a prep sheet) and Next due (Due now, or On request), Draft Now (`initiatives.draftUpdate`, which sends the Check-in chat one audience's prompt with its facts), the pencil and ×; a waiting draft shows in its row as its outbox card. + and the pencil open `AudienceSheet.tsx`: name, people from the directory, tone, the time and weekday chips, and where it goes.
- **Not yet.** A Google Doc as a target needs a write path (the calendar MCP reads Drive only): until then, a prep sheet's Copy as Markdown is the way in.

## 12. Risks and blockers

An initiative keeps a short list of what could sink it. It has three severities and no likelihood or impact scores, because it is a list to act on, not a register to maintain.

- **Blockers.** `TICKETS_HOW` (and so the ticket refresh and every check-in) reads every ticket's and sub-task's issue links (`LINKS_HOW`, `risks.ts`). It keeps only Jira's "Blocks" type (Relates, Gantt and the rest don't block work) as `links` on the ticket, together with the other ticket's status, whether or not that ticket is under the epics. A linked story outside the epics that stays To Do while its sub-tasks move is judged by its sub-tasks. `blockersOf` combines a ticket's own "is blocked by" links with the read tickets that say they block it, preferring the status of a ticket that was read. The tickets table shows each blocker as a chip in the row (`TicketBlockers.tsx`): the key and its status, red when it is outside the initiative and grey once it is done. A click opens the blocker in Jira.
- **The check-in** (`RISKS_HOW`) adds risks, updates them and closes them with the why, writing a `[risk]` line in `timeline.md` for each. It gives every open outside blocker a risk with `blocker` set to its key, and raises a decision suggestion when a risk needs the owner's call.
- **Outside blockers, always.** After every turn in one of the initiative's chats, before the commit, the daemon (`RiskKeeper.settle`, `risk-keeper.ts`) raises a risk for each open outside blocker of an open ticket that no risk names yet (`blockerRisks`; a risk names a blocker by its `blocker` or by the key in its title, in any state, so a risk the owner closed stays closed). Each one also gets a `[risk]` line.
- **The owner's edits stay.** `initiatives.saveRisk(slug, { id?, title, why?, severity, owner?, mitigation?, state })` → `{ id }` adds a risk (with no `id`) or changes one, and closing or reopening is the same call. The owner's values go into the risk's `owned`, which wins over the agent's fields (`riskOf`; an empty string is a field they cleared). The call writes a `[risk]` line and commits `<slug>: risk N added` or `<slug>: risk N changed`. The daemon also mirrors each owned risk in `~/.beluga/initiatives/<slug>/risks-owned.json`, outside the folder the chats write to. After every turn, `withOwnerEdits` restores any `owned` that the turn changed or dropped, and brings back an owned risk that the turn removed. The brief tells the agent never to touch `owned` and to suggest a change instead.
- **The page.** The Risks section (`RisksSection.tsx`) sits under the progress bar. It lists the open and watched risks, high first (`openRisks`), each with its severity, title, why, owner, mitigation, linked sources, a Watching badge and "Edited by you" on owned ones, plus Edit (`RiskSheet.tsx`) and Close. Closed ones stay behind Show Closed · N, latest closed first, with Reopen. Add Risk opens the same sheet.
